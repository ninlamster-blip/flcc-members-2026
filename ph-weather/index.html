        Where this comes from. Forecast and air quality from Open-Meteo — free, keyless and open, with weather from national meteorological services and air quality from the Copernicus atmosphere model. The app asks for a coordinate and gets a forecast back; nothing about you is sent anywhere. The radar map is the one thing that comes from somewhere else: RainViewer, which publishes national radar sweeps as map tiles, over a base map of OpenStreetMap's own tiles.

        Where it stops. This is not an official warning service and it is not PAGASA. The flood and landslide readings are rainfall applied to published thresholds — they do not know your street's drainage, the river level, what a dam upstream is releasing, or how steep the slope above you is. For warnings that account for those, follow PAGASA, PHIVOLCS and your local DRRMO.

        Everything this app remembers — your place, your units, your text size and the last reading — stays in this browser under ph/v1/.
